# Polk County (District 5C) Judges — Retention Profiles

_How retention works._ Iowa judges are appointed through a nomination process, and voters later decide whether each should be retained. Judges do not run against opponents. A judge first stands for retention at the election after one year of service. After that, regular terms are eight years for Supreme Court justices and six years for Court of Appeals and trial court judges. The bar association says appearing on the ballot "is part of this regular schedule, not an indication of concerns about a judge's performance" [1]. Judicial elections are held at the general election [8]. District associate judges are appointed by the governor from nominees of the district judicial nominating commission, the same way as district judges [7]. The Bleeding Heartland article says that if a judge gets more "no" than "yes" votes, the next governor fills the vacancy [4].

_Who is on your ballot._ The Clive 4 sample ballot lists 14 District 5C judges under "District 5C Judges" and "District 5C Associate Judges" [9]. The Des Moines Register counts eight district judges and six associate judges [3]. The Court of Appeals judges on the same ballot (Greer, Sandy, Schumacher) are not covered in this file. The ballot gives no ratings or commentary; it lists only names and Yes/No boxes [9].

_The ratings source._ The ratings come from the Iowa State Bar Association (ISBA) 2026 Judicial Performance Review, prepared by the ABA Center for Bar Leadership. The survey went to all 9,671 licensed Iowa attorneys in August and September 2026. 964 attorneys evaluated at least one judge by Sept. 14. Responses were anonymous. Attorneys were told to rate a judge only if they had meaningful professional contact with that judge [1].

The review's own cautions [1]:
- "The retention percentage reflects survey support for retaining the judge; it is not an election result."
- Scores are averages on five-point scales.
- "Response counts vary by judge, and small differences in average scores should be interpreted cautiously."
- "No attempt was made to determine an overall rating for each judge or to compare one judge's scores with those of another judge."
- "Respondents' assessments were not independently verified."

Bleeding Heartland's author also calls the ratings subjective and not a scientific assessment [4]. The ratings reflect lawyers' views, not those of litigants, defendants or victims.

This file reports the figures and the bar's published biographies. It does not recommend how to vote.

## District judges

### Scott J. Beattie
- **Appointed:** 2018 [1]. **Education:** Cedarville College (1989); Drake Law (1992) [1].
- **Background:** Before the bench, he served on active duty in the U.S. Navy Judge Advocate General's Corps, was an assistant Des Moines city attorney, and was a partner at Peddicord Wharton LLP [1].
- **Bar survey:** 96.6% retention, 145 respondents. His category averages run from 4.3 (promptness of rulings) to 4.7 [1].
- **Notable case:** On Sept. 23, 2026, Beattie dismissed a lawsuit by President Trump, U.S. Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks and former state Sen. Brad Zaun against the Des Moines Register and pollster J. Ann Selzer over the 2024 Iowa Poll. The plaintiffs claimed the poll was consumer fraud. Beattie ruled that an opinion poll cannot be called fraudulent for being wrong, in part because polls are "inherently uncertain," and that the poll is protected by the First Amendment [5]. FIRE, which represented Selzer, said the suit was about "intimidating perceived critics into silence" [5]. The plaintiffs' response and any appeal were not found in the sources read (see Open questions).

### Sarah E. Crane
- **Appointed:** June 2018. Also named to the Iowa Business Specialty Court in Nov. 2019 [1].
- **Education:** Gustavus Adolphus College (2004, summa cum laude); University of Chicago Law (2007) [1].
- **Background:** Private practice in Chicago, then law clerk to the Iowa Supreme Court, then Davis Brown Law Firm in Des Moines [1].
- **Bar survey:** 96.3% retention, 161 respondents (the most respondents of any 5C judge). Averages run from 4.5 to 4.7 [1].

### Celene M. Gogerty
- **Appointed:** 2018 [1]. **Education:** Drake (journalism, 1992); University of Iowa Law (1995) [1].
- **Background:** Prosecutor with the Linn County and Polk County Attorney's Offices until her appointment [1].
- **Bar survey:** 89.2% retention, 148 respondents. Averages run from 4.2 (perception of factual issues; temperament) to 4.5 [1]. She was not among the top- or bottom-rated lists in the Bleeding Heartland article [4].

### Samantha J. Gronewald
- **Appointed:** Dec. 2017 [1]. **Education:** University of Iowa (2000); Drake Law (2003) [1].
- **Background:** Private practice before her appointment [1].
- **Bar survey:** 97.4% retention, 152 respondents. Averages run from 4.5 to 4.7 [1].

### Christopher Kemp
- **Appointed:** District associate judge in July 2019; district judge in Jan. 2024 [1].
- **Education:** Carleton College (2006); Drake Law (2009) [1].
- **Bar survey:** 96.7% retention, 121 respondents. Averages run from 4.5 to 4.8 [1].

### Coleman McAllister
- **Appointed:** April 2019 [1]. **Education:** University of Missouri (1990); Missouri Law (1993) [1].
- **Background:** Law clerk in the Fifth Judicial District, four years in private practice, then about 20 years as a prosecutor, including Sioux County Attorney (2007–2014) and an area prosecutor with the Iowa Attorney General's Office (2014 until appointment) [1].
- **Bar survey:** 98.5% retention, 137 respondents. Averages run from 4.6 to 4.8 [1].

### Lawrence P. McLellan
- **Appointed:** 2012 [1]. **Education:** Drake (B.A. 1979; J.D. 1983); LL.M. in dispute resolution, University of Missouri (2006) [1].
- **Background:** Clerked for U.S. District Judge Ronald Longstaff, then civil litigation practice at Bradshaw, Fowler, Proctor & Fairgrave (1984–2000) and Sullivan & Ward (2000–2012), including mediation [1].
- **Bar survey:** 98.1% retention, 156 respondents. Averages run from 4.5 to 4.8 [1].

### Joseph W. Seidlin
- **Appointed:** District associate judge in Aug. 2013; district judge in Nov. 2018. He previously served eight years as a Polk County magistrate [1].
- **Education:** Drake (1982); Drake Law (1985). He was in private practice for 28 years [1].
- **Bar survey:** 96.4% retention, 138 respondents. Averages run from 4.6 to 4.7 [1].

## District associate judges

### Kimberly Ayotte
- **Appointed:** Feb. 2019 [1]. **Education:** Iowa State (1993); University of Iowa Law with Distinction (1996) [1].
- **Background:** Staff attorney at the Youth Law Center for 23 years before her appointment. She is certified as a Child Welfare Law Specialist [1].
- **Bar survey:** 85.7% retention, 63 respondents. Averages run from 3.9 (temperament and demeanor) to 4.3 [1].

### Jordan Brackey
- **Appointed:** 2024 [1]. **Education:** University of Iowa (2004); Michigan State University College of Law (2007, magna cum laude) [1].
- **Background:** Assistant Webster County Attorney (2009–2015), then assistant Polk County Attorney until his appointment [1].
- **Bar survey:** 98.0% retention, 50 respondents (the fewest of any 5C judge, so each rater weighs more). Averages run from 4.6 to 4.7 [1].

### Brendan L. Greiner
- **Appointed:** 2018 [1]; Bleeding Heartland says by Gov. Reynolds [4]. **Education:** Iowa State; Drake Law. He is also a solicitor of England and Wales [1].
- **Background:** Assistant county attorney in Polk and Story Counties, where he specialized in prosecuting vehicular homicides and fraud [1].
- **Bar survey:** 63.7% retention, 102 respondents, the lowest of the 14 Polk County judges and one of three statewide below 70% [1][3]. His averages run from 3.1 (temperament and demeanor) to 4.2 (punctuality). Other low scores: courteous and patient 3.2; avoids undue personal observations or criticisms 3.3; decides cases on law and fact 3.4; attentiveness 3.7; perception of factual issues 3.7 [1].
- **Criticism:** The only criticism found is the survey itself. No named critic, complaint or case was found in the sources read. The Register said it reached out to the three lowest-rated judges through the Iowa Judicial Branch for comment [3]. No response from Greiner was published in the sources read.
- **Context in the review:** The review says small differences in scores should be read cautiously and that it does not compare judges [1]. Bleeding Heartland's author noted that some judges have few raters [4]. Greiner's 102 respondents are more than any other Polk associate judge (50–67) [1].

### Lynn Poschner
- **Appointed:** May 2018 [1]. **Education:** University of Northern Iowa (2003); Drake Law, High Honors (2007) [1].
- **Background:** Private practice before her appointment [1].
- **Bar survey:** 100.0% retention, 52 respondents. Averages run from 4.5 to 4.8 [1].

### Ashley Stewart
- **Appointed:** 2024 [1]. **Education:** University of Houston (2003); Thomas Jefferson School of Law (2009) [1].
- **Background:** Criminal defense solo practitioner, claims specialist at Nationwide Insurance, and assistant appellate defender with the State Appellate Defender's Office [1].
- **Bar survey:** 88.9% retention, 54 respondents. Averages run from 4.1 (knowledge and application of the law) to 4.5 [1].

### Tabitha L. Turner
- **Appointed:** Aug. 2019 [1]. **Education:** Oregon State (B.S. Psychology, 2005); Drake Law (2011) [1].
- **Background:** Sole practitioner at Turner Law Firm P.L.L.C., focused on criminal defense, family law and mediation [1].
- **Bar survey:** 91.0% retention, 67 respondents. Averages run from 4.2 to 4.7 [1].

## Open questions / couldn't verify
- **Notable cases:** Only Beattie's case is described, because it was the one raised in secondary coverage. I did not search for rulings by the other 13 judges, so the absence of a case here does not mean there are none.
- **Beattie appeal:** I did not find the plaintiffs' response to the dismissal or whether an appeal was filed.
- **Greiner:** I found no explanation for his scores, no public response, and no complaint or disciplinary record. I did not check the Iowa Judicial Branch's or Judicial Qualifications Commission's discipline records directly.
- **Appointing governors:** The governor is inferred from the appointment date for every judge except Greiner. The ISBA biographies give years or months, not names.
- **Ballot headings:** The sample ballot PDF's extracted text did not show which heading Brackey, Stewart and Turner appear under. Confirm on the printed ballot.
- **Reference to non-bar evaluations:** I did not look for judicial evaluations by groups other than the bar association.
- **Other recommendations:** Some advocacy groups publish their own retention guides (for example, Iowa Right to Life lists the judges in a voter guide [10]). I did not read or evaluate those guides.
