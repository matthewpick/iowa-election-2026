# Iowa Attorney General — Candidate Profiles

_What the office does._ The attorney general is Iowa's chief legal officer, with "general charge, supervision and direction of the state's legal business," and is elected every four years [2]. The office gives legal advice to and represents most state departments, boards and officials. It represents the state in Iowa district courts, Iowa appellate courts, federal courts and the U.S. Supreme Court. It also handles criminal prosecutions and appeals, consumer protection, anti-fraud and anti-corruption work, and civil rights enforcement [2]. Iowa Capital Dispatch lists the term as 4 years and the salary as $123,669 [1].

_Ballot order / candidates:_ Brenna Bird (Republican), Nate Willems (Democrat). **Bird is the incumbent.** She was first elected in 2022, when she defeated longtime Democratic AG Tom Miller [1].

## Brenna Bird (Republican)

### At a glance
- **Age / home:** 50; lives on her family farm near Dexter [1][3]
- **Current role:** Iowa Attorney General since January 2023 [1]
- **Education:** B.A., Drake University; J.D., University of Chicago [1]
- **Career:** Chief of staff to former U.S. Rep. Steve King; legal counsel to Gov. Terry Branstad; Guthrie County Attorney (elected 2018); Iowa AG (2023–present) [1][3]

### Background & career
Bird grew up on an Iowa farm and was homeschooled (self-reported) [3]. Her campaign says that as Branstad's counsel she helped lead the administration's legal action against the Affordable Care Act and against EPA rules [3]. Iowa Capital Dispatch says she served as Guthrie County Attorney starting in 2018 [1]. Her campaign says she has been a prosecutor since 2016 and that county attorneys elected her president of the Iowa County Attorneys Association [3]. She lost the 2010 AG race to Miller, then beat him narrowly in 2022 [1][10]. IPR reports that she endorsed Donald Trump before the 2024 caucuses, attended his New York criminal trial, and was praised by him at an October 2026 Sioux City rally [10].

### Track record
- **Multistate and federal litigation:** IPR reports that Bird has joined or led many multistate lawsuits against Biden administration policies on immigration, environmental rules and other issues. She has also backed Trump administration actions, including efforts to end birthright citizenship for some U.S.-born children and the National Guard deployment to Los Angeles [10]. Iowa led a lawsuit against the 2024 federal nursing-home staffing rule [6][11].
- **Immigration enforcement:** She is defending SF 2340 (2024), which makes it a state crime for a person who was previously deported or denied admission to re-enter or be found in Iowa. Courts have blocked the law since 2024. On Oct. 22, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court asked the U.S. Solicitor General for the federal government's views on Iowa's petition, *Bird v. Iowa Migrant Movement for Justice*. Her office called this "a win." The Court has not yet decided whether to hear the case [21].
- **Tech lawsuits:**
  - *TikTok:* She sued under the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act over the app's "12+" age rating. In March 2024 she asked the court to force a "17+" rating while the case proceeds [27]. On Jan. 23, 2026, the Iowa Supreme Court ruled the state could proceed under the Consumer Fraud Act [22]. The original filing date was not confirmed.
  - *Roblox:* She sued on Dec. 16, 2025. The suit alleges the company told parents the platform was safe while it enabled sexual exploitation of children. Roblox called the claims "patently false" [20].
  - *OpenAI:* In October 2026 Iowa was one of eight states that filed separate suits. Iowa's suit alleges ChatGPT was marketed as safe for children despite allegations that it encouraged suicide and violence [10].
  - *Meta:* Her office says she sued Meta in April 2026 and that the case settled for $126 million. Of that, $25 million goes to Iowans harmed by Meta's practices toward children, $10 million to a fund that will help create a Special Victims Unit, $10 million to school grants, $11 million to a consumer-enforcement fund, and about $49 million to the state general fund (announced Sept. 14, 2026) [29].
- **Cold case unit:** Launched in 2024 to investigate more than 400 unsolved homicide and missing-person cases [14]. Bird says it has helped bring charges in five murder cases, one of them 37 years old [10].
- **Antisemitism task force:** Created in May 2024 to work with law enforcement and to provide education and training [23].
- **Victim-compensation pause:** After taking office in January 2023, Bird paused reimbursement of emergency contraception for sexual-assault victims during an audit of victim services. In a 2023 Iowa Press interview she said she intended to end it permanently [26]. On May 31, 2024, after a 16-month audit, she said payments would resume, including 362 pending claims. She said no victims had been denied or delayed services. Abortion costs would not be covered [19].
- **Staffing and prosecutions:** She says the office has doubled its number of prosecutors (self-reported, rally remarks) [10]. Willems says the office has won one election-fraud conviction. Radio Iowa reports juries acquitted defendants in four election-fraud cases [11].
- **Environmental enforcement:** In 2024 her office settled cases against NEW Co-op (an ammonia release and fish kill) and AgriStar ($50,000, wastewater). It did not bring a case against POET over its Shell Rock plant [15]. See Criticisms below.

### What they're running on
Bird asks voters to judge her record of supporting crime victims and law enforcement. She also cites defending constitutional rights, holding tech companies accountable and protecting Iowans from fraud [1]. At the Oct. 9 Iowa Press debate, she named continuing the cold case unit as her top priority. She also plans a special victims unit for crimes against children and human trafficking, funded with Meta settlement money [14][29]. She stresses her experience as a prosecutor. In the debate she told Willems, "you have never prosecuted a criminal case in your life" [11].

### Positions on key issues
- **Election integrity:** Supports voter ID. Says there is "no acceptable amount of cheating" [11].
- **Wage theft:** At the State Fair she said these are mostly federal violations for federal enforcement [9].
- **Nursing-home staffing:** Says the Biden-era mandates "would have shut down many rural nursing homes" [11].
- **Steel plant (Mesabi Metallics):** Says the office stands ready to help the state enforce the incentive agreement [11][14].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Fundraising:** From July 15 to Oct. 14, 2026, she raised just over $4.6 million and spent $7.9 million, ending with $3.5 million cash on hand [18]. More than $3.57 million since July, about three-quarters of the period's total, came from the Republican Attorneys General Association [18].
- **Contributions from POET's CEO:** He gave $25,000 (Sept. 2024), $25,000 (Sept. 2025) and $50,000 (May 2026) [15].
- **Endorsements:** President Trump endorsed her (KGAN headline; article not read) [32]. Trump also praised her at an October rally [10]. Search excerpts also show an NRA endorsement and a Maggie's List endorsement, and a past announcement of 71 sheriff endorsements. The dates of those were not confirmed and may be from 2022 [33][34]. No official endorsement page from her campaign was found.

### Criticisms & responses
- **POET pollution:** A Willems ad says Bird "refused to go after" POET and later took $100,000 from its CEO [6]. KCRG's fact check found the AG may pursue civil action beyond a DNR fine but never did. It also found the donations came after the DNR's $10,000 fine, and that Bird's Democratic predecessor took no further action after a similar 2022 POET fine [15]. *Response:* Her chief of staff said the DNR assessed the statutory maximum, the matter "was not referred to our office," and the office "applies the law equally regardless of politics" [15]. POET's own lawyers, in an Aug. 28, 2026 letter posted on Willems's site, call the ad defamatory and demand a retraction. They say POET self-reported the problem, signed a DNR consent order on July 5, 2024, and that testing showed levels below health-risk thresholds. They say the CEO's donations are unconnected to any office decision [31].
- **Emergency contraception:** Willems [6], Auditor Rob Sand [26], and Democrats and abortion-rights advocates [19] criticized the pause. *Response:* Bird said it was part of an audit and that no victim was denied services [19].
- **Nursing-home suit:** Willems questioned why the AG would sue to block staffing standards [11]. *Response:* Bird said the mandates would have closed rural nursing homes [11].
- **National focus:** Willems says the office spends too much time on political lawsuits [9][10]. *Response:* Supporters say the suits defend Iowa against federal overreach [10].

## Nate Willems (Democrat)

### At a glance
- **Age / home:** 47; Mount Vernon; grew up in Anamosa [1][5]
- **Current role:** Labor lawyer and partner at Rush & Nicholson, P.L.C., Cedar Rapids, a workers'-compensation firm [1]
- **Education:** B.A., Georgetown University; J.D., University of Iowa [1]
- **Career:** Regional director for Howard Dean's Iowa caucus campaign; Iowa House, District 29 (2009–2013 sessions; elected 2008 and 2010); full-time labor lawyer since 2010 [1][5][24][25]

### Background & career
Willems's father was a Main Street attorney and his mother a public-school teacher. He and his wife Maggie, a teacher and volleyball coach, have three daughters (self-reported) [5]. He serves on the executive board of the Hawkeye Area Labor Council, AFL-CIO, and guest-lectures at the University of Iowa Labor Center (self-reported) [5]. His campaign says he has represented building trades, UFCW, SEIU and AFSCME members, as well as law enforcement [6].

### Track record
**Legal practice (self-reported unless noted):**
- $15 million recovered for about 11,000 University of Iowa Hospitals & Clinics workers over systematically late or withheld wages [5][6]. He has also described this case in public remarks [9].
- $1.7 million in back pay for custodial workers [5][6]
- An arbitration award his campaign calls the largest collectively bargained wage increase in Iowa history, for Carter Lake police [5][6]
- A federal-court win on pension credits for UFCW Local 431 members, and a case that returned outsourced jobs [6]
- He has never prosecuted a criminal case. Bird raised this in the debate, and he did not dispute it [11].

**Iowa House (2009–2013):**
- Committees: Labor (vice chair, 2009–10), Judiciary, Education (ranking member, 2011–12), Ways and Means, State Government, and the Justice System and Education appropriations subcommittees [24]
- **SF 340 (2009), sex-offender law overhaul:** Voted yes on final passage (93–3) [6]. Voted no on amendment H-1712, which would have required mandatory electronic monitoring after release. That amendment failed, with 66 no votes. The final law left monitoring decisions to the Department of Corrections case by case and created the public sex-offender registry website [16].
- **2010 workplace breastfeeding bill:** Proposed an amendment requiring a written breastfeeding agreement with the employer at least 30 days before the due date. He later changed "notarized" to "witnessed." He said the change would help the bill pass the House. The bill died at the end of the session [17].
- **Voter ID:** Bird says he voted against voter ID as a legislator [11]. A Bird-aligned Iowa GOP post says he voted against a "voter suppression bill" (advocacy) [35]. I could not locate the specific roll call in the House Journal. I found no Willems statement on that vote. He says he will enforce current voter ID law [9].

### What they're running on
- **Wage-theft recovery unit:** A "first-of-its-kind" unit inside the AG's office to sue employers that intentionally or repeatedly violate labor law. It would seek liquidated damages and attorney fees, which he says the Department of Inspections, Appeals and Licensing (DIAL) does not pursue. It would include a direct reporting line for workers and would work with county attorneys on criminal charges [8]. He cites an estimate that $900 million a year is lost to wage theft in Iowa [8][9].
- **Polluter enforcement:** Seek penalties above the DNR's $10,000 cap. Refuse contributions from companies under investigation or found guilty of polluting. Publish an annual report and database of referred pollution cases. Notify nearby residents when a settlement is reached [6][7].
- **Consumer protection:** Rebuild the consumer protection division, with a focus on scams targeting seniors and nursing-home safety [4][10].
- **"Focus on Iowa":** "I am not running for attorney general because I want to sue the government every 10 minutes" [10]. He says he would still sue when Iowans face demonstrable harm [10].
- **Public safety:** Make sure every county attorney can prosecute the most violent felonies [9]. Keep Bird's cold case unit [10]. He says he supports the office's career prosecutors [11].
- He acknowledges his plans would need more resources and buy-in from the legislature [10].

### Positions on key issues
- **Voter ID:** Will enforce current law [9]. Says one fraud case is "one too many" [11].
- **Wage theft:** Says state law and the AG's office have a role alongside federal enforcement [9].
- **Nursing-home staffing:** Opposed the suit against federal standards [11].
- **Tech and child safety:** Credits Bird's suits against Meta. Says child online safety should stay a priority [10][11].
- **Steel plant:** The AG should make sure Mesabi Metallics "lives up to the promises" made to Iowans [14].
- **Reproductive health:** The 19th reports he has not focused on reproductive health care during the campaign [28].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Fundraising:** From July 15 to Oct. 14, 2026, he raised about $3.12 million and spent $4.1 million, ending with about $1.7 million cash on hand [18]. He received $1.5 million from the Democratic Attorneys General Association, nearly half of the period's total [18].
- **Endorsements (campaign's own page, self-reported):**
  - *Organizations:* Iowa Federation of Labor AFL-CIO, SEIU Healthcare MN & IA, UAW, Teamsters Local 238, International Association of Machinists, Sierra Club, Carter Lake Peace Officers Association, Justice for All PAC [30].
  - *Officials:* The page lists more than 50 current and former Iowa officials, including former Attorney General Tom Miller (Bird's 2022 opponent), former Reps. Dave Loebsack and Bruce Braley, former Lt. Govs. Patty Judge and Sally Pederson, and the Iowa House Democratic leader [30].
  - The Democratic Attorneys General Association also endorsed him (May 6, 2026) [36].

### Criticisms & responses
- **"Far-left" record:** Bird's campaign called him "a far-left liberal with a dangerous and radical record" [9]. *Response:* He points to his worker cases and his representation of police [6][11].
- **Sex-offender monitoring ad ("Terrifying"):** KCRG found the ad "leaves out critical context." Willems voted for the final law, which includes monitoring and created the registry [16]. *Response:* His campaign quotes a Republican sponsor who urged lawmakers to reject the amendment on constitutional grounds [6].
- **Breastfeeding ad:** KCRG found the ad accurate that he proposed the requirement, but added that he later softened it and the bill never became law [17]. *Response:* His campaign said he worked with two Republicans, including now-GOP chair Jeff Kaufmann, and that Bird is "too focused on divisive political attacks" [17].
- **No prosecutorial experience:** Raised by Bird [10][11]. *Response:* He says he would rely on the office's career prosecutors [10][11].
- **POET ad:** POET's attorneys say his "Shell Rock" ad falsely implies a bribe and demanded a retraction and apology by Aug. 31, 2026 [31]. *Response:* His site still carried the claims on Nov. 2 [6]. KCRG found the donations and the lack of an AG case to be accurate, and noted that the DNR did not refer the matter [15]. No lawsuit was found.
- **Gun rights:** Bird accused him of opposing gun rights [10]. No specific vote was cited, and none was found.

### Where they differ most
The candidates disagree most about what the office should prioritize. Bird emphasizes criminal prosecution, victim services, suits against tech companies, and challenges to federal policy or defenses of state law. Willems would shift resources toward civil enforcement for workers, consumers and the environment, and would file fewer national lawsuits. They also take opposite positions on the federal nursing-home staffing rule and on whether wage theft belongs with the state AG. Their backgrounds differ as well: Bird has prosecuted cases, and Willems has worked as a civil labor lawyer. They agree on continuing the tech and child-safety cases, enforcing the steel-plant agreement, and keeping the cold case unit [10][11][14].

### Debates & forums
- **Iowa Press (Iowa PBS), Oct. 9, 2026:** The candidates' only joint appearance [11][12][13]. Topics included election-fraud prosecutions, voter ID, nursing-home staffing, prosecutorial experience, the steel plant and social-media cases [11][12].
- **Iowa State Fair, DMR Political Soapbox, Aug. 2026:** Separate appearances [9].
