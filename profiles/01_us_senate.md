# U.S. Senate (Iowa) — Candidate Profiles

_What the office does:_ Iowa elects one of its two U.S. senators this year, for a six-year term with a $174,000 base salary [2]. The Senate shares lawmaking power with the House. It also gives "advice and consent" on presidential nominations and has "the sole power to conduct impeachment trials" [1].

_Ballot order / candidates:_ Ashley Hinson (REP), Josh Turek (DEM), Thomas Laehn (LIB), as listed on the Clive 4 sample ballot [4]. **Open seat:** Sen. Joni Ernst (R), first elected in 2014, is not running again, so no incumbent is on the ballot [3].

## Ashley Hinson (Republican)

### At a glance
- **Age / hometown:** 43 [20][24]. Grew up in Des Moines (Valley High School, West Des Moines, 2001) and lives in Marion [24].
- **Current role:** U.S. representative for Iowa's 2nd District since 2023; represented the 1st District from 2021 to 2023 [2].
- **Education:** B.A. in broadcast journalism, University of Southern California [2][24].
- **Career:** KCRG-TV reporter, producer and anchor from 2005 to 2015 [24]. Iowa House District 67 from 2017 to 2021 [2]. U.S. House since 2021 [2].

### Background & career
Hinson joined KCRG-TV in Cedar Rapids in 2005. Over about a decade she reported and anchored the morning and midday newscasts, and she won two Midwest regional Emmy Awards [24]. She has said that covering rising health care costs and the 2008 Eastern Iowa floods pushed her toward politics [24]. She left television in 2015 and won Iowa House District 67 (Hiawatha, Robins, Cedar Rapids, Marion) in 2016 with 62.5% of the vote [24][6]. Her campaign says that as a state representative she "worked successfully to balance the budget, cut taxes, and protect the most vulnerable" [6]. In 2020 she unseated Democratic U.S. Rep. Abby Finkenauer [24]. Her campaign lists her committees as House Appropriations, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and Ethics [6]. In the June 2 primary she beat Jim Carlin 74.0% to 25.7% [3].

### Track record
- **2025 reconciliation law ("One Big Beautiful Bill Act"):** Voted yes. KCRG's fact check notes that the Congressional Budget Office projects the law will increase the number of uninsured Americans by about 10 million by 2034, and that KFF estimates 60,000 to 100,000 Iowans could lose coverage. The law also created a $50 billion rural hospital fund [14][15]. Her campaign says the law cut taxes on tips, overtime and Social Security and sent "more than $209 million for rural healthcare in Iowa" (self-reported) [3].
- **American Rescue Plan (Feb. 27, 2021):** Voted no; the bill passed 219–212 [32].
- **Infrastructure law (Nov. 2021):** Voted no. PolitiFact reported she later joined a letter seeking Iowa lock-and-dam money from the law [33]. Her spokesperson said she "opposed the infrastructure package because it was tied to trillions of other spending in the House" [34].
- **ACA subsidies (Jan. 2026):** Voted against a House measure to extend the enhanced ACA premium tax credits. The measure passed the House but failed in the Senate [14].
- **Farm bill (March 2026):** Voted for the farm bill that passed the House [15]. She says her PRECISE Act on precision-agriculture technology was included in it (self-reported) [20].
- **E15 (May 13, 2026):** Spoke on the House floor for the Nationwide Consumer and Fuel Retailer Choice Act, which would allow year-round E15 sales nationwide. It passed 218–203 [25].
- **Iran war powers (Sept. 2026):** Voted against a Democratic-backed resolution to limit President Trump's war powers in Iran. Days later she called for an "immediate and successful" end to the war [9].
- **Attendance:** GovTrack data show she missed 5 of 283 House floor votes in 2026 (1.1%) [12].

### What they're running on
At her campaign launch Hinson pledged to be Trump's "strongest ally" in the Senate. She named border security, deportations, keeping transgender women out of women's sports, support for farmers, and "strengthening Medicaid, Medicare and Social Security" as priorities [2]. She says her first Senate bill would make the no-tax-on-tips, no-tax-on-overtime and reduced Social Security taxes permanent [20]. Other self-reported priorities include:
- cracking down on insurance claim denials and finishing pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) reform
- tax-free first-home savings and permitting reform
- year-round E15 and fertilizer-market transparency
- lower child care costs [20]


### Positions on key issues
- **Cost of living:** Make the 2025 tax cuts permanent. Lower energy and input costs. Pursue permitting reform and child care help [20][11].
- **Health care:** Defends the Medicaid work requirements as stopping "people who are gaming the system" [11]. Introduced the Health Insurance Transparency for Patients Act [11]. Says she will not support Social Security benefit cuts and backs a bipartisan solvency commission [11].
- **Farm bill / agriculture:** Voted for the House farm bill; supports E15 [15][25]. Her campaign is endorsed by the Iowa Farm Bureau and Iowa Corn Growers PACs [15]. Says the China Select Committee is "holding bad trade partners accountable" [11].
- **Immigration:** Supports deporting "dangerous criminal illegals" and funding for CBP and ICE, including ICE body cameras [11]. Opposed the 2024 bipartisan border bill, saying it would admit too many people [9]. Says the border was fixed by "a new president," not legislation [10].
- **Abortion:** "I am pro-life and pro-family… [I] have always supported commonsense exceptions for rape, incest, and the life of the mother." Supports over-the-counter birth control and access to IVF [20]. She did not directly answer The Gazette's question on federal "personhood" legislation [20].
- **Trump:** Endorsed by Trump in every race since 2020 (self-reported) [5]. In the debate she said: "I'll be President Trump's ally when it is important for Iowa. But I will push back on him" [11].
- **Eminent domain / pipelines:** "These decisions need to be made at the local level… landowners and local residents must have the final say" [20].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Endorsements:** President Trump [5][9]; Sen. Joni Ernst (Jan. 10, 2026) [3]; Iowa Farm Bureau and Iowa Corn Growers Association PACs [15]; the Border Patrol agents' union [10].
- **Funding:** FEC data as of Oct. 14, 2026 show $14.15 million in receipts and $2.17 million cash on hand [3].
- **Outside spending:** $4.3 million for her, led by Americans for Prosperity Action at about $3.4 million. $10.3 million against her, led by VoteVets at about $6.5 million [3].

### Criticisms & responses
- **Health care cuts.** A VoteVets ad says Hinson "voted to cut coverage for 100,000 Iowans" and pushed rural hospitals "to the brink." KCRG found the coverage estimate matched KFF's range of 60,000 to 100,000. The hospital figure relied on data from before the law, and a July 2026 update put Iowa's at-risk rural hospitals at 8 [14]. *Response:* Hinson says the work requirements target fraud and people in the country illegally, and her campaign points to the $50 billion rural fund [11][3].
- **Personal wealth and stock.** Turek says Hinson is "up to 15 times more wealthy" and still owns up to $5 million in private stock [11]. *Response:* Hinson says she and her husband sold their individual stocks in 2021, that the gain in wealth came from the sale of his business, and that she voted to ban congressional stock trading [9][11].
- **Pipeline donor ties.** Laehn says carbon-pipeline backer Bruce Rastetter is "bankrolling Hinson" and links that to her support for the 45Q and 45Z credits [13]. *Response:* The Hinson campaign did not respond to Iowa Capital Dispatch [13].
- **Iran war votes.** Turek says Hinson "voted seven times to continue" the war [21]. *Response:* She says another resolution was unnecessary and could keep the president from an "orderly end to this war" [11].
- **Accuracy of an ad.** PolitiFact rated "False" a June 2026 Hinson ad saying Turek supports sex changes for minors [28]. *Response:* Her campaign said the ad used "gender and sex interchangeably as is the case under Iowa law and nowhere do we mention surgery" [28].

## Josh Turek (Democrat)

### At a glance
- **Age / hometown:** 47; Council Bluffs [21][9].
- **Current role:** State representative, **Iowa House District 20**, since Jan. 9, 2023. He serves on the House Health and Human Services Committee and its appropriations subcommittee [26]. The Iowa Legislature's official record lists him in the House, not the Senate [26].
- **Education:** B.A., Southwest Minnesota State University; master's degree, DeVry University [2].
- **Career:** Professional and Paralympic wheelchair basketball player. Assistive-technology professional [21]. The Legislature lists his occupation as "Non-Profit Director" [26].

### Background & career
Turek was born with spina bifida, which he attributes to his father's Agent Orange exposure in Vietnam, and had 21 surgeries by age 12 (self-reported) [21][7]. He won Paralympic gold medals with the U.S. wheelchair basketball team in 2016 and 2021 [2]. He describes himself as a four-time Paralympian [21]; Ballotpedia lists him as a three-time medalist [3]. He says he ran for office after seeing a "1,000% increase" in denials and delays under Iowa's privatized Medicaid while providing mobility devices (self-reported) [21]. He is the first permanently disabled member of the Iowa Legislature [2]. His campaign says he won his first race in 2022 by six votes and was re-elected in 2024 by five points while Trump carried the district by eight [3]. He beat Zach Wahls in the June 2 primary, 62.6% to 37.3% [3].

### Track record
- **Bills he cites (self-reported):** He says he passed bills to strengthen the veterans homestead tax credit (2023), to ease Medicaid-covered wheelchair repairs (2024), and to protect service animals in rental housing (2024) [7].
- **Bills he sponsored that did not become law (self-reported):** a $25 insulin cap, a repeal of Iowa's abortion ban (HF 2455), a minimum wage increase, limits on private equity buying homes, right to repair, country-of-origin labeling, and a repeal of school vouchers [7].
- **Income-tax amendment:** Iowa Capital Dispatch reports he voted **against** the 2024 amendment language (HJR 2006) [23]. Ballotpedia's party tallies for that House vote on March 26, 2024 were Democrats 0 yes, 35 no, 1 not voting [31]. On the 2026 passage of SJR 11 (House, May 2, 2026), Democrats were 0 yes, 21 no, 12 not voting [31]. The House roll call for final passage of SJR 11 (May 2, 2026; 57–21, 22 absent) lists Turek as absent [37]; his March 26, 2024 vote on HJR 2006 is recorded as no [38]. Hinson's campaign says he "voted against advancing Iowa Amendment 1" [22]; votes on amendments to SJR 11 were not checked.
- **Immigration (2024):** Broke with his party to vote for a state law letting Iowa police arrest people who re-entered the country illegally [9][10].
- **Eminent domain (2025):** Says he "voted in favor of the ban on eminent domain" for pipelines (self-reported). Gov. Reynolds vetoed that bill [21][13].
- **Transgender-related votes:** Voted against the 2023 school restroom law, the 2023 parental-consent law on pronouns, and the 2025 law removing gender identity from the civil rights code (SF 418). Also voted against the 2025 health budget, which barred public funding for gender-affirming surgery [27].
- **Attendance:** Missed or did not vote on 58.1% of House floor votes in 2026, the second-highest rate in the chamber. The missed votes include the April 27 right-to-repair bill (HF 2763) [12]. His campaign says he cast 81% of votes over his whole career [12].

### What they're running on
Turek calls his platform "prairie populist" [7]. On costs and wages he proposes:
- a federal ban on food price gouging
- a ban on private equity buying single-family homes and farmland
- a higher minimum wage indexed to inflation
- the PRO Act [7]

On health care and anti-corruption he proposes:
- restoring the Medicaid and SNAP funding cut in 2025, and a public option [7][2]
- banning corporate PAC money and congressional stock trading
- term limits for Congress and the Supreme Court
- overturning *Citizens United* [7][2]

He also proposes ending federal 14(c) subminimum-wage certificates and raising Social Security income and asset limits for people with disabilities [2]. He said he agrees with Trump that "we should have no tax on tips and overtime" [23].

### Positions on key issues
- **Cost of living:** End the Iran war, roll back the "chaotic" tariffs, cut middle-class taxes [21]. Lift the Social Security payroll-tax cap [11].
- **Health care:** "Health care is a human right." Restore Medicaid funding, protect and expand the ACA, cap drug prices, create a public option, and bar insurers from denying doctor-prescribed care [7][21].
- **Farm bill / agriculture:** Would have voted to advance the Senate farm bill and criticized Senate Democrats for blocking it [15][11]. Supports federal right to repair, mandatory country-of-origin labeling, and antitrust action in agribusiness [7].
- **Immigration:** "Safe and secure borders" plus "an earned pathway" to citizenship and farmworker visas [11][10]. Deport people here illegally who commit crimes. Regulate and hold ICE agents accountable [7].
- **Abortion:** Restore *Roe v. Wade*; opposes federal personhood legislation [21]. Protect contraception and IVF [7].
- **Trump:** Willing to work "with anyone, including President Trump" on the border [11]. Opposes Trump's tariffs and the Iran war [21].
- **Eminent domain:** Opposes eminent domain "for corporate usage" [21][7].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Endorsements:** Iowa Farmers Union, its first federal endorsement in at least 14 years [15].
- **Corporate PACs:** Says he takes no corporate PAC money (self-reported) [7].
- **Funding:** FEC data as of Oct. 14, 2026 show $24.29 million in receipts and $3.07 million cash on hand [3].
- **Outside spending:** $9.9 million for him, almost all from VoteVets. $0.47 million against him [3].

### Criticisms & responses
- **Missed votes.** Hinson says Turek "skipped 58%" of 2026 votes, including the Iowa farm bill, while collecting a full per diem of more than $20,000 [12][3]. *Response:* "I was around meeting Iowans all over the state." He calls himself one of the most bipartisan and effective legislators and says Hinson missed 193 votes [12]. KTIV found his comparison set one year of his floor votes against six years of Hinson's floor and committee votes [12].
- **Taxes / Amendment 1.** The Iowa GOP and Hinson say his 2024 vote shows he favors "higher taxes" [16][22]. *Response:* His campaign says he "repeatedly worked with Republicans to cut taxes for Iowans, lowering income and property taxes" [16]. It also cited Common Good Iowa's statement calling the amendment "a threat to Iowa's future" [22].
- **Transgender ad (Oct. 2026).** Hinson says his ad, which said he opposes "males competing in female sports" and taxpayer-funded "sex changes," misrepresents his record [27]. One Iowa Action, an LGBTQ+ advocacy group, condemned the ad as a policy shift [27]. *Response:* His campaign says he opposed the 2023 restroom law because it put disabled Iowans' opposite-sex caregivers at legal risk, and opposed the 2025 health budget because it ignored Medicaid waitlists [27].

## Thomas Laehn (Libertarian)

### At a glance
- **Age / hometown:** 44; Jefferson (Greene County). Grew up in Allison [17][19].
- **Current role:** Greene County Attorney since 2019, now near the end of his second term. He is the first Libertarian elected to a partisan office in Iowa [2][3].
- **Education:** B.A. in political science and philosophy, Drake University (2004, summa cum laude). M.A. (2008) and Ph.D. (2010), Louisiana State University. J.D., University of Iowa (2017) [18][19].
- **Career:** Assistant professor of political science, McNeese State University. Assistant Greene County Attorney (2017). Elected County Attorney in 2018 and re-elected in 2022 [17][13].

### Background & career
Laehn is the son of a Lutheran minister [19]. After Drake he briefly worked as a public-policy analyst in Des Moines, earned his graduate degrees at LSU, and held a one-year visiting research fellowship at Hebrew University in Jerusalem [19][17]. He then taught American politics, political thought and constitutional law at McNeese State University in Lake Charles, La., for four years [17]. The Greene County site lists a 2021 book he wrote, published by Lexington Books [18]. He entered law school in 2014, where he was president of the Federalist Society chapter, and became assistant county attorney in May 2017 [19]. The Libertarian convention nominated him for Senate on May 2, 2026 [3].

### Track record
His public record is local, so there are fewer documented votes or actions than for the other two candidates. What is documented:
- **As County Attorney (self-reported):** He says he "aggressively prosecuted violent crimes" and moved most county civil work in-house, which had previously been outsourced to private firms. He also says he wrote a county public-records policy, moved the office to paperless filing, and partnered with a nonprofit on probation services [19].
- **Data centers:** Wrote Greene County's zoning ordinance for data centers [17].
- **Pipeline eminent domain:** Says Iowa House members asked him to draft legislation limiting eminent domain for hazardous-liquid pipelines. A broader version passed the Legislature in 2025 and was vetoed by Gov. Reynolds [13].
- **Cases he reported to supervisors (May 18, 2026 minutes):** His office prosecuted Christene Thompson, convicted of first-degree murder, "without any assistance from the Attorney General's office," which he called "almost unheard of in a county of our size." Nolan Wilson agreed to plead guilty to child endangerment resulting in death, a Class B felony. A third murder case (Sebastian Ramirez) was pending. The court denied James Exline's postconviction-relief application; Exline remains in prison on a 75-year sentence. The office had 65 indictable cases pending [35].
- **Postconviction relief (Sept. 2026):** A judge ruled for Laehn before trial in a postconviction-relief claim by Shelly Esquer, who was convicted by a jury in December 2024 on two Class D felonies and argued her defense lawyer was not competent [36].
- **Transition:** He is not seeking another term as county attorney; the minutes say he leaves office at the end of the year [35].

### What they're running on
- **Pipelines:** End the federal 45Q and 45Z tax credits, which he says would make the Summit carbon pipeline "collapse" [13].
- **Eminent domain:** Pass a federal law under Section 5 of the 14th Amendment banning eminent domain except for "bona fide public use" [17].
- **Campaign finance:** Overturn *Citizens United* and curb PACs and super PACs [17][8].
- **War powers:** Require congressional approval for war and cut off funding for undeclared wars [17].
- **Debt:** Vote against debt-increasing spending and support a balanced-budget amendment [8].
- **Marijuana:** Decriminalize it; he says that would be his first bill [8].

He was not included in either televised debate. Nexstar said he did not meet its criteria [9][29].

### Positions on key issues
- **Cost of living:** Calls inflation the top issue. Would end the Iran war, eliminate Trump's tariffs, and pass a balanced-budget amendment [17].
- **Health care:** Opposes more federal cancer-research funding, calling it "a matter for the states and the private sector" [17]. No stated position on Medicaid or the ACA was found.
- **Farm bill:** Would tie crop insurance and commodity supports to water-quality and soil-health practices [17].
- **Immigration:** Secure the border and "raise or… eliminate artificial caps" on legal immigration [8][17].
- **Abortion:** Personally "prolife" and would support state bans, but says Congress has no authority to regulate abortion and opposes federal personhood legislation [17][8].
- **Trump:** Criticizes Trump's tariffs, the Iran war and debt growth, and the "imperial" presidency more broadly [17][8].
- **Education:** Would eliminate the U.S. Department of Education [17].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Endorsements:** None found.
- **Funding:** FEC data as of Oct. 14, 2026 show $83,667 in receipts and $9,035 cash on hand [3].
- **Outside spending:** None reported for or against him [3].

### Criticisms & responses
No significant published criticism of Laehn by his opponents or by news outlets was found in general-election coverage. This reflects less coverage of him overall, not a finding about his record. KTIV noted that he has not served in the Legislature or Congress, so he has no comparable voting record [12].

### Where they differ most
- **The 2025 reconciliation law:** Hinson voted for it and wants to make its tax provisions permanent. Turek would reverse its Medicaid and SNAP cuts while keeping some provisions, such as no tax on tips and overtime [20][7][23]. Laehn frames federal spending mainly around the debt and a balanced-budget amendment [8].
- **Immigration:** Hinson emphasizes enforcement and opposed the 2024 Senate border bill. Turek pairs enforcement with an earned pathway to citizenship [11][10]. Laehn favors raising or eliminating legal-immigration caps [17].
- **Abortion:** Hinson identifies as pro-life with exceptions, and Turek wants *Roe* restored as federal law [20][21]. Laehn is personally pro-life but says Congress has no role [17].
- **Trump and the Iran war:** Hinson has pledged to be Trump's ally while saying she will push back [2][11]. Turek and Laehn both oppose the tariffs and the Iran war [21][17].
- **Pipelines:** Laehn makes ending the carbon-pipeline tax credits a central issue [13]. Hinson calls eminent domain a local decision [20]. Turek opposes eminent domain for corporate use [21].
- **Shared positions:** Hinson and Turek both say data-center siting should be decided locally and oppose a federal moratorium. Both also opposed lifting sanctions on Russian diesel [10].

## Open questions / couldn't verify
- **Turek's chamber:** **Settled.** The Iowa Legislature's official site lists Turek as a state representative for House District 20 in both 2023–25 and 2025–27 [26]. WOI's reference to the "State Senate" appears to be an error.
- **Turek's vote on the 2026 amendment resolution (SJR 11):** Resolved on final passage: the roll call lists him as absent [37]. His 2024 "no" on HJR 2006 is confirmed by the roll call [38]. His votes on amendments to SJR 11 were not checked.
- **Turek's medal count:** Sources differ. He says he is a four-time Paralympian with two golds [21]; Ballotpedia says three-time medalist [3]. Only the 2016 and 2021 golds are confirmed by Iowa Capital Dispatch [2].
- **Hinson's age:** Iowa Capital Dispatch lists 41 [2]; The Gazette and WHO-13 list 43 [20][24]. This profile uses 43, which matches a June 27, 1983 birth date (Wikipedia, secondary; not confirmed on bioguide).
- **Hinson's Iowa House record (2017–21):** Not found in detail. Specific bills and votes were not retrieved from legis.iowa.gov; only her campaign's general claims are cited [6].
- **"Life at Conception Act":** A third-party guide says Hinson sponsored it. This was not verified on congress.gov and is not included above.
- **Hinson's 2021 votes:** The American Rescue Plan "no" is from a House Clerk excerpt [32]; the infrastructure "no" is from PolitiFact and ABC excerpts [33][34], not the Clerk roll call.
- **Laehn's record:** His Lexington Books title and his views on Medicaid, the ACA and Social Security were not found. Case details come from county minutes and one local radio story, not court records.
- **Hinson's fundraising totals:** Ballotpedia reported $6.75 million raised as of June 30 and $14.15 million in FEC receipts as of Oct. 14. The second figure may include transfers from her House committee; this was not checked [3].
