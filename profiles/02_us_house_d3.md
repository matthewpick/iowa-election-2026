# U.S. House, Iowa District 3 — Candidate Profiles

_What the office does:_ The U.S. House of Representatives makes and passes federal laws. Each representative serves a two-year term for one congressional district, and among other duties introduces bills and resolutions, offers amendments and serves on committees, which also oversee federal agencies [1]. The seat pays $174,000 a year [2]. District 3 covers mainly south-central Iowa, including Des Moines, West Des Moines, Winterset and Ottumwa (21 counties) [4][13].

_Ballot order / candidates:_ Zach Nunn (REP), Sarah Trone Garriott (DEM). **The seat has an incumbent:** Nunn is seeking a third term [2][3]. A Libertarian, Marco Battaglia, withdrew [11].

---

## Zach Nunn (Republican)

### At a glance
- **Age / home:** 45; lives in Ankeny. He grew up on Des Moines' east side and went to Southeast Polk High School [2][3][13].
- **Current role:** U.S. Representative, Iowa District 3, since January 2023 [11].
- **Prior offices:** Iowa House District 30 (2015–2019); Iowa Senate District 15 (2019–2023) [11].
- **Education:** B.A., Drake University (2002); master's in military operational art and science, Air Command and Staff College; master's in international relations and national security studies, University of Cambridge (2007) [2][11].
- **Military:** Colonel, U.S. Air Force Reserve [2][13].
- **Family:** Married to Kelly; six children, two of them adopted [13].

### Background & career
His official House biography says Nunn served almost two decades in the Air Force as an airborne intelligence officer. In that role he flew reconnaissance missions off the coasts of Russia and China. After 9/11 he deployed three times to the Middle East and logged more than 700 combat hours [13]. He was director of cybersecurity policy on the White House National Security Council [2][13]. Ballotpedia also lists work at the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, SimSpace Corporation, and Drake as an adjunct professor [11]. He unseated Democrat Cindy Axne 50.3%–49.6% in 2022 and was re-elected 51.8%–47.9% in 2024 [11].

### Track record
- **Iowa legislature:** Nunn says he "helped pass the state's largest tax cuts three times" (self-reported) [13]. That includes the 2022 law creating a flat income tax, cutting corporate taxes and ending state tax on retirement income [23]. On abortion, he supported a six-week ban with exceptions for rape, incest and the mother's health. In a 2022 GOP primary debate, he raised his hand to say all abortions should be illegal without exception [23].
- **2025 tax and spending law (H.R. 1, "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"):** He voted aye on July 3, 2025 (roll call 190), when the House concurred in the Senate amendment 218–214. Two Republicans and all Democrats voted no [27]. He promotes its tax cuts, its no-tax-on-tips and overtime deductions, and its $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program. Iowa received $209 million from that program in its first year [4]. KFF estimates the law could leave about 80,000 more Iowans uninsured by 2034, about 68,000 of them because of Medicaid changes [4].
- **ACA subsidies:** In January 2026 he was one of 17 House Republicans, and the only Iowa Republican, to vote for a three-year extension of the enhanced ACA tax credits. Those credits had expired at the end of 2025 [14]. He told a conservative group, "I want the ACA gone as much as any of you, but I'm not going to leave 126,000 Iowans to wait for Congress" [14].
- **Funding and defense:** He voted yes on the March 2025 full-year funding bill, the November 2025 bill ending the 43-day shutdown, and the FY2024 and FY2025 defense authorization bills [11][14]. On the Iran war, he voted against two earlier war powers resolutions that passed the House (June 3 and July 23, 2026; roll calls 199 and 282). On Sept. 15, 2026, he was one of seven Republicans who voted for one, which passed 220–204 (roll 307) [5][17][18].
- **Farm bill:** He sits on the House Agriculture Committee. The House passed the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 by 224–200 on April 30, 2026. It is pending in the Senate. Nunn's office says the bill includes 21 of his priorities (self-reported) [19]. Year-round E15 passed the House but has stalled in the Senate [3].
- **Other bills:** He promotes the Stop Insider Trading Act (H.R. 7008). Congress.gov lists Rep. Bryan Steil (R-WI) as sponsor, so Nunn is a cosponsor, not the sponsor. The bill bars members, spouses and dependents from buying individual stocks and requires 7–14 days' public notice before a sale. The House passed it 232–198 on July 22, 2026 (roll 280), and the Senate failed to invoke cloture 53–47 on Sept. 30, 2026 [24][28]. His rural housing provisions became law in July 2026 as part of the ROAD to Housing Act [3].
- **Epstein files:** He voted for the final Epstein Files Transparency Act in 2025. He did not sign the discharge petition, and he voted against earlier attempts to force the files' release [6][11].
- **Committees (119th Congress):** Financial Services (vice chair of the National Security and Illicit Finance subcommittee), Agriculture, the Select Committee on the CCP, and the Congressional-Executive Commission on China [13].

### What they're running on
His campaign says "Zach Nunn Gets It Done" and lists constitutional rights, economy, border and law enforcement, families, defense, agriculture and "Iowa values" (self-reported) [7][11]. ICD says he emphasizes bipartisanship, defense and veterans, and the 2025 tax law [2]. In the Oct. 27 KCCI debate, the race's only debate, he cited his ACA vote, rural health money, housing reform, biofuels and E15 [3].

### Positions on key issues
- **Health care:** Says the ACA "is not the best way to move forward" and calls for reform. Says Trone Garriott backs "a government takeover of healthcare" [3][20].
- **Taxes:** Wants to keep the 2025 tax cuts and says they help grow the economy [3][20].
- **Iran:** Says Iran must never have a nuclear weapon and that Congress should help provide "an off-ramp" [3].
- **Water quality:** Favors a local approach and the narrower federal "waters of the U.S." rule; declined to say whether he would change the nitrate limit [3].
- **Abortion:** His campaign calls him "a recognized pro-life leader" [11].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Endorsements:** Ballotpedia lists President Trump [11]. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth headlined a Nunn fundraiser at the Iowa State Fair on Aug. 17 [2][5].
- **Fundraising (FEC, as of Oct. 14, 2026):** $6.12 million raised, $5.56 million spent, $620,759 on hand [11].
- **Outside spending (FEC, via Ballotpedia):** About $1.36 million against him, led by House Majority PAC ($754K) and AFSCME ($605K) [11].

### Criticisms & responses
- **Medicaid and clinic closures:** Trone Garriott ties his H.R. 1 vote to clinic closures in Ottumwa, Centerville and south Des Moines [4][5]. **Response:** Nunn points to rural health funding. He says the law protects benefits for seniors, children and single parents while cutting waste and fraud [4]. MercyOne cited changing demographics for the Ottumwa closure [4].
- **Iran war votes:** Trone Garriott says he "voted five times to keep it going." Her count includes a Pentagon funding vote [5][17]. **Response:** Nunn says he trusted the administration while peace talks were under way and changed his vote when that changed [3].
- **Epstein files:** Democratic ads attack his earlier votes. **Response:** Nunn says those proposals would have exposed victims' names [6]. Trone Garriott says he acted only "when the president had given him permission" [6].
- **Tax claim:** PolitiFact rated "Mostly False" his claim that Republicans passed "the largest tax cut in U.S. history." It ranks the law tied for sixth-largest since 1980, or third if the extended 2017 cuts are counted [15]. **Response:** His campaign pointed PolitiFact to Heritage Action [15].
- **Hegseth fundraiser:** On Sept. 11, The Steady State, a group of more than 430 former national security officials, asked the Office of Special Counsel to investigate whether Defense Secretary Hegseth's Aug. 17 endorsement of Nunn at the ticketed "Operation Top Nunn" fundraiser (admission $26–$3,500) misused his office. The complaint cites his reference to the Pentagon as "my department" and military-aircraft travel, and says it does not allege that reimbursement failed to occur [29]. Hegseth told the crowd "according to the lawyers, I'm only here in a personal capacity," and the Pentagon says he attended personally [29]. Nunn's campaign did not respond to requests for comment from ICD or the Gazette [29][31]. OSC closed a similar complaint about a May Kentucky appearance the next day [29]. The Iowa complaint's outcome was not found. Sarah for Iowa says a watchdog found a violation (campaign, unverified) [32].

## Sarah Trone Garriott (Democrat)

### At a glance
- **Age / home:** 48; born in Minneapolis in 1978; lives in West Des Moines [2][16].
- **Current role:** Iowa state senator, District 14. She is in her third Senate term and has served since January 2021 [16].
- **Prior office:** Iowa Senate District 22 (2021–2023) [16].
- **Education:** B.A. in history, College of St. Scholastica (1999); M.T.S., Harvard Divinity School (2003); M.Div., Lutheran School of Theology at Chicago (2008) [12][16].
- **Career:** Ordained Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) pastor at Faith Lutheran Church in Clive; Coordinator of Interfaith Engagement for the Des Moines Area Religious Council (DMARC) Food Pantry Network [2][16].
- **Family:** Husband, Will; two sons in West Des Moines public schools [9][16].

### Background & career
Her campaign says she paid for college working at Walmart, waiting tables and cleaning hotel rooms. She then served a year as an AmeriCorps VISTA volunteer supporting domestic violence victims, worked as a hospital chaplain, and was a pastor in rural Virginia (Martin Luther ELCA, Bergton) before moving to Iowa in 2013 (self-reported) [9][12]. In the debate she said she was a sexual assault crisis counselor [6]. At DMARC she works on food insecurity and builds cooperation among faith communities, and she co-founded the Iowa Interfaith Exchange [9][12].

**Election history:** In 2020 she flipped Senate District 22, winning 50.1%–49.8%. After redistricting she moved and in 2022 defeated Senate President Jake Chapman 51.4%–48.5%. In 2024 she held District 14 by 29 votes (20,467 to 20,438) [12]. This is her first run for federal office. She won the June 2026 primary with 99.3% after other Democrats withdrew [12].

### Track record
- **Committees:** In 2025–26 she serves on Health and Human Services (ranking member), Education, Commerce, Natural Resources and Environment, and Administrative Rules Review [16]. She was Senate Minority Whip in 2023–24 [16] and ranking member on Natural Resources in 2021–22 [12].
- **Bills (Democrats are the Senate minority; these did not advance):** She cosponsored a 2025 ban on lawmakers trading stocks (SF 557) and filed a term-limits amendment (SJR 10) [24]. Her 2026 bills include repealing the Education Savings Account program (SF 2321), insulin coverage (SF 2322), "right to repair" (SF 2330), school lunch funding (SF 2328), a $250 teacher classroom stipend (SF 2331), and a rate class for large energy users such as data centers (SF 2324) [12]. She backed "Erin's Law," a bipartisan proposal for K–6 child sexual abuse prevention guidelines [6].
- **Key votes:** She voted against the 2022 ban on transgender girls in girls' sports (HF 2416) and the 2023 school bathroom law [6]. She voted against the 2022 flat-tax law, HF 2317, on Feb. 24, 2022 (Senate Journal p. 396, as cited in a Democratic-aligned research file and by the Iowa GOP). The Senate passed it 32–16, and it cut rates to 3.9% by 2026, ended tax on retirement income and cut corporate taxes at an estimated $2 billion revenue cost [21][30]. She says she has voted for tax cuts in the Iowa Senate [20].

### What they're running on
Her campaign lists "A Higher Standard for Washington" (stock trading ban, term limits, banning corporate PAC money), lower costs, health care, immigration, public schools, land and water, public safety, reproductive rights, veterans, Social Security and Medicare, and labor rights (self-reported) [8][10][12]. In speeches and in the debate she ties higher prices, health care and the Iran war to corruption [24]. She has promised to reverse the H.R. 1 Medicaid cuts and restore the expired ACA subsidies [2][4]. On Medicaid she said in the debate that the cuts are "devastating to our neighbors" [3]. Her site says she is "not running to play the game. I'm running to change it" (self-reported) [12]. She also calls herself "the only Democrat in Iowa to flip multiple districts while representing a district won by Donald Trump" (self-reported) [9]. At a Sept. 13 event in Montgomery County she said, "We need someone who is going to protect our healthcare and restore it, not cut it" [4].

### Positions on key issues
- **Health care:** Restore Medicaid funding and protect the ACA. Add a public option alongside private plans. Let Medicare negotiate drug prices [3][12].
- **Taxes:** Make the tips, overtime and senior deductions permanent. Focus tax relief on families earning under $180,000, and opposes "tax giveaways for billionaires" [3][4].
- **Costs and trade:** End the "chaotic tariffs." Crack down on price gouging and junk fees. Expand the Child Tax Credit and EITC [3][12].
- **Iran:** Iran must never have a nuclear weapon. Remove Secretary Hegseth, and use diplomacy and sanctions [3][5].
- **Water:** Lower the federal nitrate limit for drinking water. Give farmers more conservation resources [3].
- **Immigration:** Comprehensive reform: border security, legal pathways, protection for Dreamers, and ICE accountability [12].
- **Abortion:** Restore nationwide abortion protections and safeguard contraception and IVF [12].
- **Labor:** Supports the PRO Act. Her campaign cites a 100% lifetime Iowa Federation of Labor rating (self-reported) [12].

### Endorsements & funding
- **Endorsements (via Ballotpedia):** Iowa Federation of Labor AFL-CIO, Iowa State Education Association, IUOE Local 150 and EMILY's List [12]. Her campaign says 25 Iowa Republicans endorsed her (self-reported) [8].
- **Fundraising (FEC, as of Oct. 14, 2026):** $8.07 million raised, $6.92 million spent, $1.15 million on hand [11].
- **Outside spending (FEC, via Ballotpedia):** About $2.26 million against her, almost all from the Congressional Leadership Fund ($2.25M) [11]. Total outside spending in the race topped $10.6 million (DMR/FEC, via ICD) [6].

### Criticisms & responses
- **"Transgender ideology" bill:** Nunn said she "authored the bill that would teach transgender ideology to kindergartners." **Response:** ICD found that the bill he cited concerns Area Education Agencies and does not mention sex education [6]. She says some attacks target her support for Erin's Law and for teaching children "the language to ask for help" when abused [6].
- **Transgender sports and bathroom votes:** Attack ads cite these votes, and Nunn calls her part of the "extreme element of her party" [6]. **Response:** She says every bill she faced "took away a parent's voice" [6]. She has also said she opposes men in women's locker rooms [5].
- **Taxes:** Nunn says she supports "the largest tax increase" by backing repeal of the 2025 law. The Iowa GOP says she opposed the 2022 flat tax; the Senate roll call records her voting no on HF 2317 [21][26][30]. Nunn's campaign says "Do the math" that she would repeal the "Working Families Tax Credit" [26]. **Response:** She says she has never voted to raise Iowans' taxes and has voted for tax cuts [5][20]. In the debate she said she supports making the tips, overtime and senior deductions permanent [3][20]. The 2022 vote remains the main source of the dispute, and her explanation of that vote was not found.
- **Attendance:** Nunn's campaign says she "missed one out of every five votes" in the Iowa Senate this year (campaign claim; not independently verified) [22]. No response was found.
- **Effectiveness:** Nunn criticizes her stock trading bill for not advancing. **Response:** She says it stalled because her party is in the minority [24].
- **Debate timing:** Nunn's campaign faulted her for agreeing to a debate only on Oct. 27, after early voting had begun [7][25]. ICD reports that both campaigns blamed each other over scheduling [6][25].

### Where they differ most
The clearest split is over the 2025 tax and spending law. Nunn voted for it and campaigns on its tax cuts and rural health fund. Trone Garriott would reverse its Medicaid reductions and aim tax relief at lower- and middle-income families, while keeping the tips, overtime and senior deductions that both now say they support. On health care, both backed extending the ACA subsidies. Nunn, however, calls the ACA something to reform or replace, while Trone Garriott would protect it and add a public option. On the Iran war, Nunn moved from opposing war powers limits to supporting one in September 2026, while Trone Garriott opposes the war and wants new Pentagon leadership. They also differ on abortion, tariffs, the federal nitrate standard, and how broad a congressional stock trading ban should be. Their records are different in kind: Nunn has a federal voting record and bills that passed the House, while Trone Garriott's legislative record is in a state Senate where her party is in the minority.
