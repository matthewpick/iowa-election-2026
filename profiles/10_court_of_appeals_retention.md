# Iowa Court of Appeals — Judicial Retention Profiles (Nov. 3, 2026)

_What the office does._ The Iowa Court of Appeals is the state's intermediate appellate court. It hears cases the Iowa Supreme Court transfers to it and reviews trial-court decisions. Its decisions are final unless the Supreme Court grants further review [9][10]. It has nine judges, who usually sit in three-judge panels [10]. Judge Schumacher writes that the caseload is historically split roughly equally among criminal, civil and juvenile appeals, and that the court filed 1,076 opinions in 2025 [15].

_How retention works._ Iowa has used merit selection since a 1962 constitutional amendment. The governor appoints judges, and voters later decide whether each should be retained. Judges do not face an opponent. A judge first stands for retention at the election after one year of service. After that, Court of Appeals and trial judges serve six-year terms and Supreme Court justices serve eight [1]. The ISBA says appearing on the ballot "is part of this regular schedule, not an indication of concerns about a judge's performance" [1]. The Iowa Judicial Branch says retention elections "focus on the professional competency of Iowa's judges," as quoted by the Des Moines Register [10]. A judge with more "no" than "yes" votes loses the seat and the next governor fills the vacancy (per Bleeding Heartland, a secondary source [11]). Judges are rarely voted out. The exception cited is 2010, when three Supreme Court justices were not retained [10].

_On the ballot._ Voters statewide see "Shall the following Judges be retained in office?" with a Yes/No box for each of three judges (all three were appointed by Gov. Kim Reynolds) [1][11]: **Sharon Soorholtz Greer, John M. Sandy, Julie A. Schumacher.** No Supreme Court justices are on the 2026 ballot [10].

_About the ratings used below._ The primary source is the ISBA's 2026 Judicial Performance Review, compiled by the ABA Center for Bar Leadership [1]. It was sent to all 9,671 Iowa-licensed attorneys in August–September 2026. 964 attorneys evaluated at least one judge, and responses were anonymous [1]. Attorneys were told to rate an appellate judge only if familiar with that judge's opinions [1]. Scores are averages on a five-point scale. The ISBA states that it made no attempt to produce an overall rating or to compare judges, that "respondents' assessments were not independently verified," and that the retention percentage "is not an election result" [1]. This profile has not found a published margin of error or methodology audit.

## Sharon Soorholtz Greer

### At a glance
- **Residence:** Marshalltown [1]. **Appointed:** April 2019 by Gov. Reynolds (the ISBA gives "April 2019"; Ballotpedia says April 27, 2019, succeeding Judge Christopher McDonald) [1][7].
- **Education:** B.S. in psychology and child development, Iowa State University (1978); J.D., University of Iowa College of Law (1982), where she was a member of the _Iowa Law Review_ [7].
- **Before the bench:** Practiced with Cartwright, Druker & Ryden in Marshalltown [1]. She is a fellow of the American College of Trial Lawyers, a member of the American Board of Trial Advocates and of the Iowa Academy of Trial Lawyers [1].
- **Prior retention vote:** Retained Nov. 3, 2020 with 74.2% "yes" (825,999 of 1,113,185 votes) [7][9].

### Background and appointment
Greer worked as a county-seat trial lawyer before her appointment [1][14]. Her biography lists service as co-chair of the Iowa Rules of Civil Procedure Task Force, co-chair of the Iowa Children's Justice Advisory Committee, and membership on the ISBA Jury Instruction Committee and a drug-treatment-court standards committee. She previously co-chaired the Rules of Evidence Task Force [1]. Judge Schumacher's ISBA article credits a task force that Greer led, begun in February 2025, with producing the _Standards of Practice for Lawyers Representing Children_ in child-welfare proceedings [15].

### ISBA 2026 results
90.9% retention from 230 respondents, the largest respondent pool of the three [1]. Her scores run from 4.4 to 4.6 on every measure [1]. Bleeding Heartland, a political blog, saw "no red flags" for the three judges. The ISBA itself did not rate or rank judges [1][11].

### Notable opinions and rulings
- _Straight v. Hagie Manufacturing Co._, No. 25-0326 (decided March 11, 2026): Greer wrote for a three-judge panel that included Judges Schumacher and Chicchelly. The court held that the economic loss doctrine barred a farm family's negligence claims over a broken sprayer axle and the chemicals in its tank. Claims for damage only to the product itself belong in contract law, not tort [16].
- Court records from April 2026 list Greer as presiding judge ("Greer, P.J.") on panels, including a juvenile appeal with Judges Schumacher and Chicchelly [FindLaw excerpt, 17].
- This profile did not find Greer dissents covered by news outlets. Her opinions are not systematically reviewed here. See Open questions.

### Her own stated views
In an October 2026 ISBA article, Greer wrote that having judges from county-seat and rural practices "positively enhances the judicial decision making" of the court [14] (self-reported).

### Criticisms and responses
None found from an identifiable critic or organization.

## John M. Sandy

### At a glance
- **Residence:** Spirit Lake [1]. **Appointed:** June 28, 2024 by Gov. Reynolds, to the vacancy left by Chief Judge Thomas Bower's retirement. Ballotpedia says he took office Aug. 2, 2024 [5][6].
- **Education:** B.A.s in philosophy and Catholic studies (2007) and J.D. (2010), University of St. Thomas. He was editor-in-chief of its _Journal of Law and Public Policy_ [1].
- **Before the bench:** District judge in Judicial District 3A from March 2021. Earlier, a shareholder and managing partner at Sandy Law Firm, P.C., in Spirit Lake. Earlier still, a first assistant public defender in Minnesota [1][12].
- **Prior retention vote:** None on the Court of Appeals. This is his first appearance on that ballot. This profile did not look for any district-court retention result.

### Background and appointment
In an ISBA interview, Sandy said his parents are both attorneys. He said he began college studying to be a priest, and he described plaintiff's personal-injury work as the most fulfilling part of his private practice (self-reported) [12]. In a University of St. Thomas alumni interview he said he and his wife have four children (self-reported) [13]. His biography lists service on the Iowa Judicial Branch Fairness in Jury Selection Task Force and a faculty role at the National Computer Forensics Institute [1].

### ISBA 2026 results
87.8% retention from 197 respondents, the lowest retention percentage of the three. His scores run from 4.2 to 4.4 [1]. He has the shortest time on the appellate court, since 2024, so the ratings cover less of his appellate record than the other two [1][6]. The ISBA does not discuss this.

### Notable opinions and rulings
- _In the Interest of O.W._, No. 24-0862 (Jan. 9, 2025): A father challenged the termination of his parental rights for abandonment. The panel (Chief Judge Tabor, Judges Buller and Sandy) affirmed in an opinion by Tabor. Sandy dissented in a 39-page opinion, writing that he would reverse because the evidence was not clear and convincing that the father abandoned the child [17].
- No. 25-1061 (April 29, 2026; defendant Rachel Whiteside): Sandy wrote for a panel affirming consecutive sentences for multiple sexual-exploitation counts. The court said it would not second-guess a sentencing court that grounded its decision in the nature of each offense, the harm to the victim and public protection [20].
- This profile did not check whether the Supreme Court took further review of _O.W._

### His own stated views
In an ISBA interview, Sandy said he hopes to be remembered "that I was kind" [12] (self-reported).

### Criticisms and responses
None found from an identifiable critic or organization.

## Julie A. Schumacher

### At a glance
- **Residence:** Schleswig [1]. **Appointed:** August 2019 by Gov. Reynolds, replacing retiring Judge Gayle Vogel [1][8].
- **Education:** B.A., University of South Dakota (1990), communications and English; J.D., Creighton University School of Law (1993) [1].
- **Before the bench:** Nearly 20 years in private practice in Denison. She was also a Crawford County prosecutor, assistant Denison city attorney and hospitalization referee [1].
- **Judicial path:** District associate judge, Third Judicial District, from December 2012. District judge from January 2017 (filling the vacancy left by Judge Mary Sokolovske) [1][8]. She was retained as a district judge in 2014 with 77.2% [8].
- **Prior retention vote on this court:** Retained Nov. 3, 2020 with 76.2% "yes" (856,450 of 1,124,149 votes) [8][9].

### Background and appointment
Schumacher grew up in rural Sioux County [1]. Her biography lists service as chair of the Expedited Rules of Appellate Procedure Task Force (2024–25) and membership on the State Advisory Council for Children's Justice. She previously served on the Supreme Court Grievance Commission and the Juvenile Justice Reform Task Force, and she has taught at new-judge orientation [1][15]. The Des Moines Register summarizes her path as "five years as an associate judge and two as a district judge" [10]. The ISBA dates give about four years and two and a half years. See Open questions.

### ISBA 2026 results
92.7% retention from 178 respondents, the highest retention percentage of the three. Her scores run from 4.3 to 4.6 [1].

### Notable opinions and rulings
- _In the Interest of J.B. and R.R._, No. 25-1802 (Court of Appeals, Feb. 11, 2026): The court sat en banc on a State appeal from a Polk County juvenile ruling (Judge Lynn Poschner). The court affirmed, holding that Iowa Code section 232.117(3) lets a juvenile court choose among guardian categories and weigh the children's best interests. Schumacher dissented, writing that the statute requires placement with the Department of Health and Human Services unless it waives [18]. On further review the Iowa Supreme Court unanimously vacated the Court of Appeals decision on May 8, 2026 (amended July 14, 2026). It held the statute's directive to transfer guardianship to HHS is not discretionary [19]. Schumacher's position matched the Supreme Court's result.
- _Straight v. Hagie_ (March 2026): She joined Greer's opinion [16].

### Her own stated views
In a March 2026 ISBA article, Schumacher wrote that the Court of Appeals has "prioritized focus on timely permanency for children under court jurisdiction" [15] (self-reported).

### Criticisms and responses
None found from an identifiable critic or organization.

_Where they differ most._ Their ratings are close. The practical differences are time on the court (Sandy joined in 2024, the others in 2019), prior trial-bench experience (Sandy and Schumacher had it, Greer did not), and the lowest and highest of the three retention percentages (Sandy 87.8%, Schumacher 92.7%), a spread of 4.9 points.

## Open questions / couldn't verify
- **Opinions sample:** The cases above are the ones found with limited searching. They are not a systematic or representative review of each judge's rulings. No full count of opinions, dissents or reversals on further review was done.
- **O.W.:** Whether the Supreme Court took further review of _In the Interest of O.W._ (Sandy dissent) was not checked.
- **Sandy's district-court retention vote:** The result was not found.
- **Schumacher's tenure:** The Des Moines Register says "five years as an associate judge and two as a district judge" [10]. The ISBA bio says associate judge from December 2012 and district judge from January 2017 [1]. That is about four years as an associate judge, and she was a district judge for about two and a half years before the 2019 appointment. The Register's figures were not reconciled.
- **Campaigns:** No organized campaign for or against any of the three was found. The Bleeding Heartland article describes none [11]. No endorsement lists were found. Ballotpedia says none of the three completed its 2026 Candidate Connection survey [6][7][8].
- **Judicial Branch bios:** The Iowa Judicial Branch bios were seen only in search excerpts, which match the ISBA bios used here [1].
- **Retention-vote consequences:** The "more no than yes" rule is cited to Bleeding Heartland [11]. The underlying Iowa Code provisions were not read.
