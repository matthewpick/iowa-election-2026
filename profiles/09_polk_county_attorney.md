# Polk County Attorney — Candidate Profiles

_What the office does._ Polk County's official site says the County Attorney "is responsible for prosecuting all criminal violations of state law that occur within the county" [1]. The office's 2023 status report lists its other work. A Juvenile Division handles delinquency and Child in Need of Assistance (abuse and neglect) cases. Felony bureaus handle homicide, sex crimes, trafficking, drug and gang cases, and civil forfeiture. A Civil Bureau acts as general counsel to the county and its officials and defends them in lawsuits. An Investigative Unit serves papers and manages evidence [2].

_Ballot order / candidates:_ Jonah Dyer (Republican), Kimberly Graham (Democrat) [3]. **Graham is the incumbent.** She took office on January 3, 2023 [4].

## Jonah Dyer (Republican)

### At a glance
- **Age:** 43. **Residence:** Johnston [5].
- **Hometown:** South-central Phoenix, Arizona. He is the second of six children (campaign, self-reported) [6].
- **Current role:** Criminal defense lawyer and founder of JHD Law in Des Moines [7].
- **Education:** B.A., Arizona State University (2006). J.D., Drake University Law School (2010) (campaign, self-reported) [6]. The DMR lists "Juris Doctorate" [5].
- **Political experience:** "Never sought nor held political office" [5].
- **Earlier work:** Two-year church mission in Mexico (returned in 2003). Officer with the Arizona Department of Corrections for about three years while in college (campaign, self-reported) [6].

### Background & career
Dyer's campaign says he moved to Des Moines in 2007 for law school. During law school he worked several jobs, including delivering pizza, and he kept that job for a while after he was licensed [6]. He says that in 2010 he helped classmates start a full-practice law firm. His work there focused on court-appointed criminal defense for low-income Polk County residents. In 2012 he left to open his own firm, JHD Law [6]. He says he has spent more than 15 years in court "several times a week" and has defended "thousands of Polk County residents who cannot afford to hire an attorney" (self-reported) [6][8].

The Polk County Republican Party nominated Dyer at a special convention on June 27, 2026. No Republican had run in the June 2 primary for county offices. Iowa law lets parties fill general-election ballot vacancies after the primary [7][9]. The same convention nominated Alyson DeMoss for Supervisor District 4. Party chair Andrew Ventling announced both nominations [7].

### Track record
Dyer has never held public office, so he has no voting or official record [5]. His professional record is in private defense practice, mostly court-appointed cases (self-reported) [6]. He describes his practice as "thousands of cases" over 15 years, and says that experience means he "knows exactly where the system works and where it fails" (self-reported) [8]. His only public-office-related step so far is the June 27, 2026 nomination: the Republican primary for this office was canceled because no one filed, and the party filled the vacancy by convention [7][26]. He did not complete Ballotpedia's Candidate Connection survey [26]. This research found no published appellate decisions, disciplinary records or bar ratings for him. See Open Questions.

### What they're running on
**DMR questionnaire, complete answers** [5]:
- *Why are you running?* "The citizens of Polk County deserve competence over catch phrases, substance over slogans, justice over jargon, fact over fiction, and people over politics. They deserve a county attorney whose only priorities are protecting victims, holding offenders accountable, and upholding the rule of law. Every resident of Polk County deserves to know that when they call 911, when they walk into a courtroom, or when they become the victim of a crime, the law will be enforced fairly, consistently, and without fear or favor. Those aren't red or blue values. They're Iowa values."
- *Most important issue?* "Many of Polk County's issues aren't within the purview of the Polk County attorney. Of those that are, the rise in juvenile gun violence is of particular concern. The incumbent's response to this issue keeps offenders from benefitting from the supportive and rehabilitative infrastructure the criminal justice system provides and sends a signal to other youth that gun violence will go unpunished because of their age. Youthful offenders should be brought into the system so it can work with them to develop individualized outcomes that encourage personal responsibility, growth, and change."

**Campaign "Plans for Change"** (self-reported) [10–13]:
- **Prosecuting serious crime:** He pledges to "prosecute serious crimes competently, meet critical deadlines, make sound charging decisions, and hold offenders accountable in proportion to their conduct" [10].
- **Restorative justice:** He would keep the Community Restorative Dialogue program and expand it to defendants with "a wider range of criminal histories and risk profiles." He would also try it in prisons. He would add a formal evaluation that compares participants with similar people who did not take part [11].
- **Economic burdens:** He would lobby the Legislature to end driver's-license suspensions imposed only for unpaid fines and court costs. He would also seek to make driving while barred a scheduled traffic violation with no jail time [12].
- **Marijuana diversion:** He would keep diversion but require drug education and prevention classes even when no treatment is recommended. He would "consider requiring a defined period of abstinence, verified through... drug testing" before charges are dismissed [13].

**Forums/debates:** This research found no candidate forum or debate coverage for this race.

### Positions on key issues
- **Juvenile gun crime:** Wants youthful offenders "brought into the system" rather than kept out of it [5].
- **Victims:** "Victims first." Prosecutors should communicate with victims and "never let them get lost in the shuffle" (self-reported) [8].
- **Diversion:** Supports it, with stricter completion requirements for marijuana cases [13].
- **Fines and fees:** Supports ending license suspensions for inability to pay [12].
- **Data:** Wants measurable outcome data for diversion and restorative programs [11][12].

### Endorsements & funding
- Nominated by the Republican Party of Polk County [7].
- Ballotpedia lists no endorsements for him [26]. This research found no union, police or organizational endorsements. His site takes donations through WinRed [8]. No fundraising totals were found; the Iowa campaign disclosure database was not reachable through the tools used.

### Criticisms & responses
- This research found no published criticism of Dyer by Graham or her campaign, and no Graham reply to his campaign-site pages.
- Dyer is a career defense lawyer running for chief prosecutor, and has not held office. He describes himself this way: "Jonah's an Attorney. Not a Politician." He says his defense experience shows him "exactly where the system works and where it fails" (self-reported) [8].
- Dyer's criticisms of Graham are listed under her profile. His site presents them in the campaign's own voice, not as neutral findings [10-13].

## Kimberly Graham (Democrat, incumbent)

### At a glance
- **Age:** 62. **Residence:** Des Moines [5].
- **Current role:** Polk County Attorney since January 3, 2023. She is the first woman elected to the post [4].
- **Education:** B.A., University of California-Irvine (1991). J.D. with honors, Drake University Law School (1999) [4].
- **Earlier jobs:** Food server, housecleaner, teaching assistant. Union flight attendant (APFA) for 12 years while in college and law school [4].
- **Political history:** Ran for U.S. Senate in 2020. Elected County Attorney in 2022 [5]. Unopposed in the June 2, 2026 Democratic primary (39,947 votes, 99.4%, certified) [26]. In 2022 she won the general election 56.9% to 42.8% over Republican Allan Richards (104,438 to 78,580) [27].

### Background & career
Polk County says Graham spent 23 years in private practice. Her work included court-appointed defense for low-income clients, representing parents or children in Child in Need of Assistance cases, and mediation. From 2018 to 2022 she was the attorney and guardian ad litem for children of parents in the Polk County Recovery Court program [4]. Her campaign describes 20 years as "an advocate and attorney for abused or neglected kids and parents in juvenile court" and 22 years of volunteer legal work (self-reported) [15]. In 2022 she won on a reform platform, succeeding John Sarcone (32 years in office) [16].

### Track record
- **Bail (2023):** Directed prosecutors to stop requesting cash bail for most low-level, non-violent offenses without a victim [16][2].
- **License Reinstatement Program:** In March 2023 the program moved to an ability-to-pay model [2]. Her campaign says participation is up 111% since late 2022 and driving-while-barred arrests are down 9% (self-reported) [17].
- **Marijuana diversion:** Launched March 2023. First-offense possession cases can earn a dismissal. The office reported 237 successful participants in the first nine months [2].
- **Restorative justice:** 37 victim-offender dialogues in 2023 [2]. Her campaign says more than 3,000 Community Restorative Dialogues have been held since 2023, and that "early data" show lower recidivism (self-reported) [17].
- **Other 2023 changes:** Police use-of-force cases are reviewed by the office instead of deferred to a grand jury, and the County Attorney reviews every proposal to send a juvenile to adult court [2].
- **Staffing:** The office added victim-witness liaisons and attorneys, including staff for a new juvenile courtroom [2]. In 2026 Graham said the office had "only two domestic violence prosecutors with more cases than they can handle" [18]. This research found no reporting on turnover levels.
- **Warrant Resolution Clinic (April 3, 2026):** See Criticisms. After the clinic, the Legislature banned such clinics (HF 2787). Gov. Reynolds signed the ban on June 2, 2026 [19][20].

### What they're running on
**DMR questionnaire, complete answers** [5]:
- *Why are you running?* "I'm running to continue the work of helping to create a safer, healthier Polk County, while working to eliminate racial and income disparities in our justice system. The Polk County Attorney's Office has accomplished a lot in less than four years and we have more to do. We created and expanded restorative justice programs. We improved the license reinstatement program to make it more affordable, resulting in a 111% increase in participants in the program and contributing to a 9% decrease in driving while barred arrests. We created a marijuana possession diversion program. And, together with our law enforcement partners county-wide, we continue our focus on assertively prosecuting those who commit serious or violent crimes in our community."
- *Most important issue?* "Issues are intertwined and there is more than one important issue. In general terms, poverty, mental health, substance use disorder, and homelessness are critical issues. Domestic violence, sexual assault, and human trafficking are three areas of crime that have grown in Polk County, therefore, I've requested the addition of two prosecutors to work in our Special Victims Bureau. Lastly, and tragically, the rise in the number of kids with guns continues. Together with others in our community, I've been working to bring Group Violence Intervention (GVI) to Polk County and our first training is in October 2026..."

**Campaign plans** (self-reported) [17]: two more Special Victims Bureau prosecutors; a full-time social worker; a domestic-violence lethality questionnaire; a free child internet-safety class for parents; Group Violence Intervention; expanding the Youthful Supervision diversion program.

**Forums/debates:** None found.

### Positions on key issues
- **Juvenile justice:** Her 2022 platform presumed against waiving juveniles to adult court "whenever possible" [23]. For youth gun crime she points to GVI [5].
- **Bail:** No cash bail requests for people who are not a violent threat [23].
- **Diversion, victims, disparities:** Expand diversion and restorative justice; add special-victims prosecutors; end racial and income disparities [5][17].

### Endorsements & funding
- 2022: endorsed by Iowa Citizens for Community Improvement (advocacy) [16]. Local Democratic groups have promoted her 2026 campaign.
- Ballotpedia lists no 2026 endorsements for her [27]. This research found no police-union endorsement and no fundraising totals.

### Criticisms & responses
- **Warrant clinic.** Rep. Steve Holt (R-Denison) blamed the April 2026 clinic for the killing of Ashley Marie Hall. The woman charged had a non-violent warrant resolved there a week earlier, and Holt said the office ignored her criminal history. Hall's mother agreed with Holt; her father saw no direct connection [19]. **Graham's response:** "if I believed that there was any way that not holding a warrant resolution clinic could prevent homicide, I would never hold one." She said two judges at the clinic had access to criminal histories, as they do whenever a warrant is recalled [19]. Dyer says the ban shows the "limits of her influence" with lawmakers [12].
- **Case handling (Dyer).** Dyer cites three cases [10]. On Watsabaugh, the Des Moines Register reported in Sept. 2025 that the charges were dismissed after prosecutors missed an indictment deadline. The defense attorney said the deadline expired Aug. 25 and he moved to dismiss on Aug. 26, and the Register reported that Graham apologized to police [28]. The article is paywalled; I saw only the excerpt, so I could not confirm whether charges were later refiled and did not see court records. He objects to Holmes' felony being reduced; the office says she pleaded to involuntary manslaughter, an aggravated misdemeanor, and got the two-year maximum [22]. He says Graham "dismissed the murder charge" against Brown. A March 2026 filing shows the office dropped two counts pending a final autopsy before deciding on murder [24]. Brown later pleaded to child endangerment resulting in death and received 58 years, with 35 before parole eligibility [21]. Graham's only public response I found to any of these is her apology on Watsabaugh [28]; I found none to Dyer's campaign pages.
- **Warrant-clinic outcome.** Dyer says the ban shows Graham cannot win lawmakers' support [12]. Rep. Rob Johnson (D-Des Moines) and others said 49 other participants were "on track with the judicial system" and the killing could have happened without the clinic [19].
- **Program claims (Dyer).** Dyer says the recidivism and enrollment figures measure activity, not results [11][12]. No response found.
- **Juvenile guns (Dyer).** Dyer says her approach "sends a signal... that gun violence will go unpunished." Graham points to GVI and community partnerships [5].

## Head-to-head comparison

| Issue | Dyer (R) | Graham (D, incumbent) |
|---|---|---|
| Prosecutorial experience | None. 15+ years of defense work (self-reported) [6] | Polk County Attorney since 2023 [4] |
| Earlier career | Court-appointed criminal defense, JHD Law [6] | Juvenile court/CINA attorney, GAL, defense attorney, mediator [4] |
| Juvenile gun crime | Bring youth "into the system" [5] | Group Violence Intervention; presumption against adult waiver [5][23] |
| Marijuana diversion | Keep it; add required education and possibly abstinence testing [13] | Created it in 2023 [2] |
| Restorative justice | Keep and expand it; require rigorous evaluation [11] | Created and expanded CRD; plans more [17] |
| Fines and license suspensions | Lobby to end suspensions for inability to pay; make DWB a traffic violation [12] | Ability-to-pay License Reinstatement Program [2] |
| Victim services | "Victims first"; competent charging [8][10] | +2 SVB prosecutors, lethality screening, social worker [17] |
| Endorsements found | Polk County GOP nomination [7] | Iowa CCI (2022) [16] |

**Where they differ most.** The two differ most on juvenile gun cases. Dyer wants more young offenders formally charged. Graham emphasizes keeping youth out of adult court and using GVI. They also differ on how to judge the office's record. Graham points to program participation numbers and case volume. Dyer argues those are activity measures and points to specific cases and the warrant-clinic ban. Both support diversion and restorative justice in principle. Dyer would add requirements and formal evaluation. Dyer is also proposing legislative changes on license suspensions and driving while barred, which go beyond Graham's program-level changes.
